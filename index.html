How It Works

The ocean is the largest accessible carbon reservoir on earth.

The ocean holds approximately 38,000 gigatonnes of dissolved inorganic carbon — 50 times more than the atmosphere. The chemistry that stores it is the same chemistry the DOR process exploits. The ocean already removes 25–40% of all human CO₂ emissions naturally every year. We accelerate that process with precision.

The Bullet node as a DOR reactor

The standard Bullet UMU body — 25m × 25m × 36m — is submerged at 100m depth and configured as a sealed seawater electrochemical batch reactor. Ten reactor levels, each 1,800m³. The graphene composite walls are simultaneously the structure, the seawater containment, and the electrochemical electrode. No separate electrode hardware. No reagents. No shore plant required.

Applied voltage from two Gaia power nodes above — transmitting via graphene HVDC tether — shifts the pH of the seawater inside. Dissolved inorganic carbon converts to CO₂ gas. The gas rises within the sealed reactor. Ambient ocean pressure at 100m depth — 11 bar — provides free hydraulic energy to purge the CO₂ upward to a collection point. From there it travels down a graphene pipeline to the seabed for permanent mineralisation in basalt. Geological permanence. No monitoring required after injection.

The alkalinity cascade — self-amplifying removal

The electrochemical process generates not only acid (driving CO₂ extraction) but also base — an alkaline return stream. This is returned to the ocean below the thermocline. The consequence: the treatment zone develops an atmospheric CO₂ absorption rate approximately 10× the global average. The more we extract, the faster the ocean replenishes from the atmosphere above. The system amplifies its own effect.

Permanent sequestration

Collected CO₂ is injected to the seabed and reacts with basalt and ultramafic rock to form stable solid carbonate minerals. The same process that has sequestered carbon naturally for millions of years. Complete mineralisation in months to decades. Stable on geological timescales. Verifiable by standard geochemical monitoring.

The most auditable carbon removal in history

Every cycle is logged. Every tonne is traceable. Every sequestration site is monitorable. The alkalinity cascade produces measurable ocean chemistry changes. The CO₂ pipeline carries metered gas. The mineralisation process follows confirmed geochemical pathways. This is not estimated offset. It is measured, permanent, physically confirmed removal — blockchain-verifiable, auditable by any counterparty.

Offshore Data Infrastructure

One data centre invading one citizen's life is one too many.

We saw the physical atoms behind the AI debate — the power, the noise, the water, the land — before we saw the debate itself. We aimed at the atoms.

The conversation about AI's footprint is usually about ethics, jobs, and risk. Underneath all of that is something simpler and more solvable: physical infrastructure. A modern hyperscale data centre draws as much power as a small city, runs cooling systems loud enough to disturb neighbours for miles, consumes millions of gallons of fresh water, and occupies land that communities increasingly do not want to give up.

The Universal Modular Unit — the same graphene composite shell that powers the world and removes its carbon — reconfigures into a sealed offshore data centre module. One manufacturing process. A different interior. The result removes four of the five most-cited problems with AI infrastructure entirely, simply by moving the atoms somewhere they were never a problem to begin with: the open ocean.
